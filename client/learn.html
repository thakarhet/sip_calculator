A SHORT GUIDE

Getting to know
a SIP.

A Systematic Investment Plan is a way to invest a set amount into a mutual fund at regular intervals, such as every month. It’s a habit and a schedule—not a type of mutual fund or a promise of returns.

01

Small, regular contributions

Choose an amount and a schedule that fit your budget. For example, investing ₹5,000 each month means contributing ₹60,000 over a year. The value of those investments will move with the fund and the market.

02

How units are purchased

Each instalment buys mutual fund units at that day’s applicable NAV. When the NAV is lower, a fixed amount generally buys more units; when it is higher, it buys fewer. This is often called rupee cost averaging, but it does not prevent losses or guarantee a better result.

03

Time and compounding

When investment growth remains invested, future growth can build on earlier growth. Compounding can help over long periods, while markets can also fall and returns can vary from year to year. Calculator projections use assumptions to illustrate one possible path.

04

Before you begin

Understand the fund’s objective, risk level, fees, exit terms, and the time horizon that may suit it. Keep emergency needs and high-interest debt in mind. You can review or change an SIP according to the fund and platform rules.

Remember

Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. Read all scheme related documents carefully. Past performance does not assure future results; the calculator is for education and illustration only.
